# Voice: scott

The operator's voice, for a piece marked `Voice: scott`. It admits only rules that change with whose name is on the piece. Each rule's dominant pattern comes first and any exception follows.

## Opener

- **Open with a blunt declarative statement of the core premise.** Never a question, a quote, an anecdote or a scene. The doctrine's answer-first bullet owns the rest.
- **Lead the opening sentence with a frame-setter.** `"The most valuable resource to the business is…"` is one form.
- **Keep the opening paragraph to one to three sentences and 25 to 55 words.**
- **An email opens by the Email section instead.**

## Pronouns and Voice

- **Default to "we" in technical and proposal writing.**
- **Use "I" for subjective framing.** An email follows the Email section's pronoun rule.
- **Use "you" only for instructions, never as the primary voice.**
- **Write in active construction.** Passive voice is for a third-party system's behavior.

## Reasoning Moves

- **Pivot against the status quo.** Build the status quo the reader holds, then pivot with `However,`, `By comparison,` or `Comparatively,` to why it falls short, after the answer is stated.
- **Italicize at most one word per sentence.** It stresses a magnitude, a universal or a key technical term.
- **Bold a number together with its unit.** `**X% reduction**` is one form.
- **Keep a scope-limiting caveat to one clause, as its own sentence after its claim.** `"Not every customer does."` is one form.
- **Leave out an internal decision the reader never asked about.** It explains us to a reader who wrote about their own matter.
- **End a paragraph on a short sentence of five to twelve words.**

## Heading Case

- **Case follows where the text is read.** Title Case where it displays as formatted markdown, repository markdown included, and ALL CAPS where it is read raw, such as plain text or a code comment no house style skill owns. A heading inside C# or SQL follows that language's house style skill. One case per document, held throughout.

## Page Mechanics

- **No contractions in technical documentation.** An email follows the Email section.
- **No exclamation marks in a document body.** An email places them by the Email section.
- **Two spaces follow every period.** A document, a repository markdown page and an email draft alike take them, and the kit's own prose takes one.

## Close

- **Close on the end state.** In two to four short sentences, in past tense or present indicative, state what the reader now has after applying the design. Never close on a future promise, a gut punch, a rhetorical question or a rallying cry. `"The ultimate result of this design is that we have…"` is one pattern.
- **Label the final section `END RESULT`, `Aftermath` or `Resolution`, or leave the close unlabeled.**
- **An email closes by the Email section instead.**

## Email

An email takes these rules in place of the Opener and Close sections, for a reply and a new thread alike. The answer-first order, sentence shape and structure stay the doctrine's.

- **Open a reply with a one-line thanks to the person, on its own line.** `"Thanks [Name]!"` is one form, and no comma follows "Thanks".
- **Open a new thread with a time-of-day greeting to one named person, even where others are copied.** `"Good morning [Name],"` is one form.
- **Say what was checked before giving the finding.**
- **Use "I" for what the operator did, saw or thinks, and "we" for what the operator's company does or will do, whatever the number of recipients.**
- **Mark one honest uncertainty where it applies, once.** In plain text, emphasis takes asterisks.
- **Credit Claude plainly wherever Claude did work the reader relies on.** Where Claude's own text is carried, it sits between horizontal rules as a summary it prepared. The credit is honest accounting, so a draft never drops it.
- **Soften the opening line's verb and use no contraction there.** `"We are working on adding"` over `"We're adding"`. Elsewhere an email may use contractions.
- **Group a long technical reply under bold Title Case labels, with a list beneath each, after the thanks and the frame.** A list the reader will answer is numbered, so the reader answers by number, and any other list takes bullets.
- **Short lead-ins such as `"That said…"` and `"Full disclosure…"` belong to this voice, and an ellipsis may mark the pause.**
- **Close with a thanks and a well-wish.** `"Thanks again [Name].  Have a great day!"` is one form. A `"Let me know if…"` line comes before it only where a reply is needed, and a call to action appears nowhere else.
- **Exclamation marks go on the greeting, the thanks and the well-wish.** A technical sentence never carries one.
- **Leave relationship warmth out of a draft.** An honorific or praise turns on the relationship, so the operator adds it by hand.
