# Finishing Work

An effort is done when behavior is verified, security is reviewed, documentation matches reality, and the plan doc is closed. Run these steps in order. Steps 2 and 3 may be dispatched in parallel once step 1 passes. Step 4 runs once both have returned, and step 5 follows it.

When per-section reviews cleared parts of the changeset, tell the finishing reviewers what they covered and what changed since, so their budget goes to cross-section cohesion and deltas. A section is cleared when its last review round ran its lens at Fable and ended the loop under the terminal condition in executing-work's `references/findings.md`. Its lens is the prose-reviewer where executing-work's Audience rule gives it the document pair alone, else the adversarial-reviewer. Every hunk on its files after that round, the Minor pass's included, must be body prose under `findings.md`'s definition. A fix delta reviewed below Fable leaves its section uncleared, and a prose-only one does not. Cut duplication, never coverage. For a small effort with no meaningful per-section reviews, fold the two advisory lenses into one adversarial dispatch carrying their scope. Its findings under those lens labels take step 2's advisory disposition, never the adversarial verdict.

Dispatch the advisory reviews (step 2) at dispatch table row "Finishing advisory", and step 3's document lenses, and its final adversarial review where any section is uncleared, at dispatch table row "Finishing reviews". The Fable-aimed dispatches alone take executing-work's capacity reading (its step 1) first. Fill executing-work's Reviewer Dispatch template, and where Workflow is unavailable take that file's Agent-tool route at the row's own model.

**Unavailability is the gate failing to run at full strength, with no decision behind it:** the round cannot run at its assigned tier on this account or in this environment. Confirm it from evidence, never from an expectation that a model is missing. A capacity reading that passes leaves this ladder live. Two triggers confirm it.

**The first is an actual dispatch failure:** attempt the override and read the error. A silent substitution is this trigger with no error, read per the resolved-model paragraph below.

**The second is a pair of consecutive stopped dispatches at the model in question:** the original and the one same-model retry this rule allows, each stopped by this rule in any shape. Read the model from the dispatch record you hold, never from the `.meta.json` sidecar. The pair shows only that this gate could not be run at this tier in this environment, never that the model is unreachable. A stop at another model, or a lone first stop, takes its dispatch class's own disposition.

**The resolved model lives on the transcript's assistant lines, and the dispatch record carries the requested one.** It is the `message.model` value on the `"type":"assistant"` lines of `agent-<id>.jsonl`, under the session's `subagents/` directory, or under `subagents/workflows/<run-id>/` for a Workflow round. Tally it per line from a finished run, never with a whole-file Read or a grep that prints matches: `node -e 'require("fs").readFileSync(process.argv[1],"utf8").split("\n").filter(Boolean).forEach(l=>{try{const o=JSON.parse(l);if(o.type==="assistant"&&o.message)console.log(o.message.model)}catch{}})' <transcript> | grep -v '<synthetic>' | sort | uniq -c`, or the host equivalent. Record every value it prints with its count.

An id such as `claude-opus-4-8` satisfies the requested alias when it carries that family's prefix or a stronger family's. A dispatch that passed no override requested its charter's `model:` line, or the session's own model where the charter names none. A tally with no count satisfying the alias, and at least one parsed non-synthetic line, takes trigger one. An empty tally goes to the never-started paragraph below where `<synthetic>` accounts for every assistant line, and is unreadable otherwise. Ignore `undefined` entries, and call an all-`undefined` tally unreadable.

A distribution mixing the requested model with another never enters the ladder. Record it in the final Chapter and the close-out status as a partial substitution, stated as a proportion, and let the round stand. Name a majority-substituted round as one that ran mostly below its tier, so the operator decides on a re-run. A mixed compensating dispatch is recorded the same way, never compensated again.

**The wedge hallmark is four observations of a dispatch, and all four must hold.** Its status still reads `running`. Its liveness reading has shown no sign of life for the window its shape sets, or that window has elapsed since dispatch with no usable reading. It has not answered a `SendMessage` probe within the probe window its shape sets. Its open-call reading below is zero.

The shape picks the reading and the windows. A dispatch that has taken a turn is read on byte growth, against its class's growth and probe windows. One whose pair of counts, per the never-started paragraph below, reads both zero is read on that pair, against the five-minute first-turn and probe windows. One whose instrument is unreadable is read on elapsed time since dispatch, against its class's windows. Once a probe is away, the reading is the non-synthetic assistant-line count wherever the transcript is readable, whatever the shape.

Short of all four observations, a quiet running dispatch is working. An unanswered probe is uninformative while a tool call is open, since it cannot land until the call returns. Nothing short of the whole hallmark authorizes killing any dispatch **for being quiet**, and every dispatch site points here rather than restating it. With all four, before the outer window closes, TaskStop only on the fault sign below. With no sign, hold it as working and report the readings to the operator. The outer window is four times the class's growth window. It closes that long after the last sign of life, read as the hallmark's shape reads it, and an open call is a sign at every wake that finds it open and at the first wake that finds it returned. When it closes with status still `running`, TaskStop with the silence recorded. A call open longer than an outer window is reported to the operator at each wake. A kill on other grounds, such as a brief invalidated mid-flight, a tree-mutating probe or a deliberate replacement, is executing-work's TaskStop with no window to wait out. Three kills of this rule's own need no sign, each a reading of a shape, not a silence: the stop-first TaskStop before the synthetic-only fault's re-dispatch, the never-started stop beside it, and the run-level stop below.

**The open-call reading is a set difference over the transcript's `assistant` and `user` lines, never its last line or a grep.** A parallel batch writes each `tool_use` as its own `assistant` line and returns the results on separate `user` lines in any order, so the last line reads closed while a call in the batch is still open, and a tool result can quote the string `tool_use`. Other line types repeat the blocks and are skipped. Read `node -e 'const s=new Set();require("fs").readFileSync(process.argv[1],"utf8").split("\n").forEach(l=>{let o;try{o=JSON.parse(l)}catch{return}const c=o.message&&o.message.content;if(!Array.isArray(c))return;for(const b of c){if(!b)continue;if(o.type==="assistant"&&b.type==="tool_use")s.add(b.id);else if(o.type==="user"&&b.type==="tool_result")s.delete(b.tool_use_id)}});console.log(s.size)' <transcript>`, or the host shell's JSON equivalent. A count above zero is an open call: hold the dispatch as working. A `0` is proven only by this command printing above zero at a transcript holding an open call, the session's own read inside that call. That transcript is `~/.claude/projects/<launch directory, non-alphanumerics as dashes>/<session id>.jsonl`, the id being `CLAUDE_CODE_SESSION_ID`. With no agent id or file at the dispatch's path, the observation holds on the elapsed-time fallback below. An unproven `0`, or no digit at a present file, is recorded, reported at each wake, and leaves the hallmark short.

**The positive sign of death is the fault sign, a closed list of one.** Among the transcript's `"type":"assistant"` lines the last carries `"model":"<synthetic>"`, the harness's API-error placeholder. Read it after the never-started paragraph's path control, parsing each line as JSON, as `node -e 'let m;require("fs").readFileSync(process.argv[1],"utf8").split("\n").forEach(l=>{let o;try{o=JSON.parse(l)}catch{return}if(o&&o.type==="assistant")m=o.message&&o.message.model});console.log(m==="<synthetic>"?1:0)' <transcript>`, or the host shell's JSON equivalent. A `1` is the sign: a failed model call was the agent's last act, with no assistant line after it inside the probe window. A later `user` line does not clear it, since only an assistant line shows the agent acted again.

**Transcript growth is a short-circuit, never a second vote on liveness.** Byte growth inside the class's growth window ends the inquiry with no probe spent, even with no assistant line in it. The window opens at the last observed growth, or at dispatch where none has been observed. The one exclusion is the synthetic-only shape: where no turn is yet established, read the never-started paragraph's pair of counts, never the grown bytes. The byte reading retires at the probe send. From then on, liveness is a rise in the non-synthetic assistant-line count above its value at the send.

**A zero-growth reading is an absence-proving check and earns its silence the same way any other does.** Take it as a byte count at the agent's own transcript, read as a size without contents (.NET IO through PowerShell on Windows), never by reading the file and never at a task's `.output` path. Tell a fan-out's transcripts apart by the dispatch record you hold, never by the `.meta.json`. Where you hold the directory and the agent id and find no file, the instrument is unreadable. Before a zero counts, show the instrument observing growth at a live sibling whose byte count moves between two readings. With no control, or a contradicted one, the instrument is unproven. On an unreadable or unproven instrument, take elapsed time since dispatch for the same window, go to the probe, and record the growth reading as unavailable.

Probe an Agent-tool dispatch at its task id. Probe a Workflow round's agent at its agent id inside the run directory, since `SendMessage` to the run's own task id reaches no agent. Where the agent id cannot be recovered, the probe can never be sent, and waiting on it is the multi-hour wait this rule exists to end. So at the close of the elapsed-time fallback's two windows, with status still `running`, TaskStop the run's own task id. That ends the whole round, so first let every other dispatch in it complete and have its output read. The stopped dispatch enters the ladder below like any other stop, its shape recorded as diagnosed on the fallback with the probe unsendable.

**A dispatch that never took a turn is its own shape, and the transcript names it.** Its reading is a pair of counts over the `assistant` lines: those whose `message.model` is not `<synthetic>`, the harness's placeholder for an API error, and the `<synthetic>` lines themselves. Route on the pair, since the non-synthetic count alone reads zero for both shapes. Both counts zero at the first-turn window's close is the never-started shape. A zero non-synthetic count beside a non-zero `<synthetic>` count is a transient fault. Its remedy is a stop-first re-dispatch rather than the ladder's downgrade, and it spends the ladder's one same-model retry.

Take both counts line-filtered, never with a whole-file Read, on the transcript the dispatch record's agent id names. The non-synthetic count is `grep -a '"type":"assistant"' <transcript> | grep -acv '"model":"<synthetic>"'`, and the companion swaps `-acv` for `-ac`, or the host shell's equivalents. Every grep over a transcript takes `-a`. Read the control first: a count of `"type":"user"` lines in the same file, which prints no count at all for a missing path, where the counting pipeline prints a bare `0`. The control proves the path, never the `<synthetic>` spelling.

Where the agent id is unknown or no file sits at its path, fall back to elapsed time since dispatch against the class's growth window, then its probe window, never the short windows. Record the first-turn reading as unavailable. Where the probe is unsendable, the run-level stop above owns the close of those windows. A wedge diagnosed on this fallback still counts toward trigger two's pair.

**Evaluate the hallmark on a cadence rather than on a suspicion**, for every in-flight dispatch whatever its model. The wakes are three: the completion notification for any dispatch the `WAITING:` line named, a timer wake the session armed, and the session's first return to that dispatch after either, before anything else is done with it. Take the first-turn reading at the first wake at or after the first-turn window closes, and at every wake after. A dispatch carrying a model override takes it at that wake whatever woke the session. A non-zero non-synthetic count settles it. No pair routes anywhere before the first-turn window closes. Send the probe at the first wake that finds the shape's window closed flat, and run the probe window from that send.

A session able to wake itself on a timer arms one for each named dispatch at its class's growth window before the `WAITING:` turn end. It re-arms at every later wake that leaves a window open, and never waits on a timer alone. Without a timer a wedge raises no wake, and it surfaces only when the operator next reads the session. A synchronous dispatch (`run_in_background: false`) is diagnosed after it returns, and whether to take its trade is executing-work's call.

**Set the windows from the asymmetry, which runs one way.** A TaskStop on a live agent discards its work, while waiting on a dead one costs only time, so err long everywhere and longer still for an unfamiliar class. A review-class dispatch, read-only and advisory with no build by charter, takes a growth window of fifteen minutes and a probe window of twelve. The `qa-verifier` and the `docs-curator` take thirty and twelve. Any other non-review class sets its windows from its own cadence, with at least that much room.

The never-started shape takes five and five in every class, since a turn's assistant line appends before its first tool result returns. The first-turn window runs from the dispatch time the session holds, or else from the transcript's first `timestamp` field, read as `head -1 <transcript> | grep -ao '"timestamp":"[^"]*"' | tail -1`. With both counts zero at its close, send the probe. With both still zero five minutes after that send, TaskStop and enter the ladder below. Its re-dispatch runs on these short windows if it too never starts, and on its own shape's windows otherwise.

**What follows a TaskStop is the wedged dispatch's own class.** Every class gets one same-model re-dispatch first, and where it runs, nothing is recorded against the model. The retry is one whatever route spends it. Stopped in its turn, it meets trigger two whatever the two shapes. No chain reaches the third same-model dispatch executing-work forbids.

A review-class pair compensates rather than falls back, per the route below, and a per-section reviewer aimed below its lens's ceiling re-aims one tier up per executing-work's rule instead. Compensation is open only at a tier the chain has not ruled out, so a pair stopped at opus ends the round ungated. The compensating dispatch is the last rung, and whatever ends its route ends the round ungated.

A stopped `qa-verifier` or `docs-curator` retry hands its step to the orchestrator, which runs the step's commands in the main thread, recorded as self-run. The step is ungated only where the main thread cannot run them either. Re-dispatch a stopped read-only scout or drop it, naming the gap in the Chapter. Every other class returns to its own skill's ladder, executing-work's for an implementer.

**Compensating.** Re-dispatch at dispatch table row "Gate compensation". Name `agentType` as the scoped agent the dispatch being compensated ran as, whichever Fable-aimed reviewer that was (`grimoire:adversarial-reviewer` the common one). Fill executing-work's reviewer-dispatch template, whose three fields are required. The doctrine's standing-dispatch bullet carries the operator's request for this route, so it needs no per-session ask. Await the round as executing-work's completion contract awaits any dispatch, by the `WAITING:` turn end. Keep it one round under this skill's single tree-state capture. A folded whole-changeset pass is an `adversarial-reviewer` dispatch, so name that `agentType`. Compensate only the dispatches that failed, judged one at a time.

**The bare fallback** applies where fable is ruled out and Workflow is unavailable too. It is an Agent-tool dispatch at `model: 'opus'` with the reviewers' frontmatter effort, and its record names the model and effort it ran at beside dispatch table row "Gate compensation". Never drop to the session model while an Opus override is available.

Record a compensation, a fallback or an ungated end in the final Chapter with the trigger, the chain, each stopped dispatch's shape and the evidence. Name it again in the close-out status, so I can decide on a re-run where fable is reachable. The evidence is the verbatim error, the `message.model` tally or the `<synthetic>` count pair, or for a wedge its time at `running`, liveness, open-call and fault-sign readings and the probe. A compensation reached by executing-work's capacity reading records the reader's line verbatim instead. Say whether you compensated or fell back. Never let an unavailable gate pass unremarked.

**Bracket steps 1 through 4 with the tree-state check.** Capture `git status --porcelain` before dispatching each of those rounds and compare when the round returns, before acting on any finding. A delta is the same incident executing-work's review rule names, with the same consequences, recorded in the final Chapter. The check compares porcelain readings, so it misses any write leaving no tracked-file delta, any act moving HEAD with the worktree, any act outside the worktree, and git's ref, config and remote plumbing. Step 5 stays outside the bracket, since the docs-curator's edits under `docs/` are its deliverable.

**The finishing pass has one compaction boundary rule, and this is it.** No section closes during finishing, so executing-work's step 8 never fires. The trigger is the persona module's boundary reminder arriving on a tool result between finishing steps, which needs no count. On that signal, append `### Interim board N - YYYY-MM-DD` to the plan doc with the content shape executing-work names for it, honor the commit model for the doc, and declare the boundary with `kit-compact-checkpoint.js boundary` at a banked moment, as executing-work's step 8 states. Take the boundary whenever the signal stands rather than once per pass.

## Steps

**Take in the integration branch before step 1.** Fetch, and where it moved, merge it into the working branch, surfacing sibling-session conflicts. The merge takes the lane the doctrine's merge rule names, beside step 1's gate. It applies under Branch-and-PR, under Commit-and-Push only on a worktree branch, and never under Review-Only. A branch whose pull request is based on another branch first takes step 7's stacked-parent bullet's read and retarget, so the integration branch this update fetches and merges is the one the pull request now has. It takes the base in whether or not an arm stands, since step 1's gate must read the tree that lands. The bullet's push and arm wait for step 7, where the push carrying this merge takes the state read's armed OPEN case where an arm stands.

**Establish the effort's base ref before step 1, unconditionally.** Derive it after that merge, outside every step, so the changeset listed against it is the plan's own files. Record it in the final Chapter and hand it to every dispatch and read that follows. Under Review-Only the base is `HEAD`. Under Branch-and-PR it is the merge-base of the working branch with the integration branch. Under Commit-and-Push it is the parent of the earliest commit that appended a Chapter to the plan doc. Throughout this skill, the integration branch is the one branch-hygiene's Safe Set resolves, which reads the pull request's base first, and `<integration>` is its branch name without the `origin/` prefix. So a branch whose pull request is based on another branch takes its merge-base with that branch, and the changeset holds this plan's work alone.

Under Commit-and-Push, walk `git log --reverse --format=%H -- <plan path>` and take the first hash whose `git show --format= --unified=0 <hash> -- <plan path>` output matches `^\+### Chapter `. The base is that hash's parent, resolved to a sha at derivation time (`git rev-parse <hash>^`) and recorded as the sha rather than as the caret expression. Where that hash is the repository's root commit, the base is the empty tree (`git hash-object -t tree /dev/null`). Where concurrency put a Commit-and-Push session on a worktree branch, the base is the merge-base with the integration branch instead. Where no commit has appended a Chapter, the base is `HEAD`. Never derive it from the commit that added the plan doc.

A wrong base raises no error, so check it before spending it. Compare step 5's changeset listing, taken against it, one-directionally with the union of the plan's `Files in scope:` lines. Surface only the entries outside both that union and the bookkeeping set, which is the plan doc and the docs index (`docs/README.md`). Sibling commits, untracked files and scope-less plans surface entries, not a wrong base.
